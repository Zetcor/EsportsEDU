Traditions: Rituals and Community Bonds

Tradition Slang Dictionary

Post-Game Etiquette

Typing “gg” or “gg wp” at the end of a match has become a universal sign of respect. Even in games where communication is limited, this quick gesture sends a powerful message. At the pro level, this digital etiquette is translated into physical acts—handshakes, fist bumps, or bows—that take place on-stage after matches. These moments often carry emotional weight, especially after closely fought series, where teams may exchange a quick word of admiration or even a respectful nod that says more than words could. In some tournaments, refusing a handshake or failing to say “gg” can spark controversy, reinforcing how important these rituals are to the integrity and sportsmanship of the scene.

Pre-Match Chants, Huddles, and Rituals

Esports teams frequently engage in ritualized preparation before matches, echoing age-old sports traditions. These aren’t just psychological tricks—they’re grounding mechanisms, helping teams shake off nerves, enter “the zone,” and affirm unity. Team chants—loud, rhythmic rallying cries shouted before walking on stage—are meant to fire up both the players and the crowd. For example, teams like T1 or Fnatic may have custom chants in their native languages that become recognizable across tournaments. Pre-game huddles, where players lock arms or gather in a tight circle backstage, are used to exchange final words of encouragement, remind each other of strategies, or just reaffirm trust. These moments are often captured by broadcast cameras, giving fans a glimpse of the emotional buildup before a showdown. Ritual objects like plushies, flags, or good-luck charms frequently appear in team booths or on-stage desks. Some teams have mascots they bring to every LAN event. For instance, a player might wear the same hoodie to every match, or tap a certain item before sitting down—a personal superstition that becomes team lore.

Victory Poses and Emotes

Winning a match is often celebrated with flair, and esports has its own catalog of signature celebrations. In-game, players may use emotes—pre-designed animations that perform dances, poses, or taunts—immediately after clutch moments or match wins. Titles like Fortnite, Valorant, and Overwatch have made emotes central to player expression. On-stage, it’s common for players to develop personalized victory poses. These might include:

Victory Poses

  • Holding up the team flag
  • A signature fist pump or hand sign (like Faker’s iconic hand wave)
  • Pointing to the crowd or standing up with arms outstretched
  • Mimicking their in-game characters or acting out dramatic reenactments of the final moment—a mix of humor and pride that makes fans cheer even louder

Legendary Plays and Infamous Fails

Certain moments in esports history become more than highlights—they become community myths. These "forever clips" are replayed endlessly, dissected by analysts, and turned into memes that persist for years.

  • A "1v5 clutch" where a lone player defeats an entire enemy squad is a feat that electrifies casters and fans alike. It's a moment of genius under pressure—like ZywOo in CS:GO, TenZ in Valorant, or Faker in LoL.
  • A "throw" happens when a team has the lead and then makes a series of poor decisions, handing victory to the other side. These moments are equal parts painful and iconic, with casters often yelling, "They’ve thrown it!"
  • Iconic fails, such as misclicks, disconnects, or accidental ultimates, are burned into collective memory. In Dota 2, a misused Black Hole can haunt a player's legacy for years.

Inside Jokes & Memes

Esports culture thrives on humor, timing, and community-driven references. Inside jokes are more than just punchlines—they’re cultural touchstones passed around in streams, on social media, and in crowded arenas. Whether born from unexpected upsets, infamous misplays, or repeated phrases from casters, these moments get immortalized as memes that define fan culture and create a sense of belonging among viewers.

  • “It’s not over till it’s over” becomes a meme when underdog comebacks shock the world.
  • “Xpeke backdoor” refers to one of League of Legends’ most famous game-winning moves—so well-known it’s now a verb.
  • “Lag excuse” is a tongue-in-cheek defense used when someone performs poorly—often spammed ironically in Twitch chat.

Nicknames & Catchphrases

Nicknames and catchphrases in esports are a blend of admiration, myth-making, and community creativity. Often coined by casters or fans after iconic performances, these labels become part of a player’s legend. They serve as shorthand for greatness, style, or clutch performance—and fans rally behind them with pride. From chants in stadiums to digital tributes, these names become inseparable from the players themselves.

  • “The Unkillable Demon King” (Faker)
  • “King of Clutch” (s1mple)
  • “The Professor” (Daigo in fighting games)
