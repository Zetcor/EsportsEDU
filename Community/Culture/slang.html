A Guide to Gaming Terms

Gaming slang is the unofficial language of players around the world. Built from years of competitive play, streaming culture, and online communities, these terms capture the humor, intensity, and camaraderie of the gaming world. This dictionary offers quick definitions and context for common phrases, helping readers navigate in-game chat, forums, and streams with confidence.

A

  • AFK – Away From Keyboard; used when a player is inactive or temporarily leaves the game. Commonly used in multiplayer games to let others know the player won’t be participating for a while.
  • Aggro – Short for “aggression,” referring to a character or player attracting enemy attention, often by dealing damage.
  • Aim Assist – A feature in shooter games that helps players’ crosshairs automatically align with targets, especially useful for controller players.
  • AoE – Area of Effect; refers to attacks or abilities that affect multiple enemies or a specific area instead of a single target.
  • Adapt – A term used when a player changes their strategy or playstyle mid-game to respond to unexpected challenges from opponents

B

  • Bot – Refers to a non-human player controlled by the game’s AI, or a human who plays at a very low skill level, often seen as an easy target in competitive play.
  • Buff – A game mechanic that improves a character, weapon, or ability, typically through patches or updates to make them stronger or more effective.
  • Baiting – A tactic where a player lures an opponent into a vulnerable position by appearing to be weak or unprotected, only to ambush them.
  • Ban – A penalty where a player is temporarily or permanently removed from a game, server, or platform due to violating rules or engaging in disruptive behavior such as cheating, harassment, or exploiting bugs.
  • Backdoor – A strategy where a player sneaks into an opponent’s base or area, bypassing defenses to achieve a surprise victory.

C

  • Camping – Staying in one spot for a long period to wait for enemies to pass by, often used in shooters or survival games.
  • Clutch – A situation where a player turns the game in their favor, usually under high pressure, by performing outstanding plays when the team is on the verge of losing.
  • Cooldown – The time a player must wait before they can use an ability or skill again.
  • Carry – A player who performs exceptionally well and leads the team to victory, often by dealing the most damage or securing key objectives.
  • Combo – A series of moves or attacks executed in quick succession, often used in fighting games or MOBAs to maximize damage or disable an enemy.

D

  • DPS – Damage Per Second; a measurement of how much damage a character or weapon can inflict over time. Players with high DPS are crucial in many team compositions.
  • Debuff – A negative status effect placed on a player or enemy that weakens their abilities or stats, such as slowed movement or reduced damage.
  • DC – Disconnected; used to indicate that a player has lost connection to the game, which often leads to a disadvantage for their team.
  • Dodge – The act of avoiding an incoming attack or ability, typically by moving out of the way. Many games have a dedicated dodge mechanic to evade damage.

E

  • ELO – A rating system used to rank players based on their performance, commonly used in games like League of Legends and Chess. A higher ELO indicates a stronger player.
  • Emote – An in-game animation or expression that allows players to show emotions or reactions, often used in celebration or taunting.
  • Exploiting – Using unintended mechanics or bugs within a game to gain an unfair advantage. Often leads to criticism when discovered.
  • Event – A special, time-limited occurrence within a game that may offer rewards, such as seasonal events or special tournaments.
  • Elite – A term for highly skilled players or teams that perform at the top level. Often used to refer to competitive players or high-ranked individuals.

F

  • FPS – First-Person Shooter; a genre of games where the player experiences the action from the perspective of the protagonist’s eyes, such as Call of Duty or Overwatch.
  • Feed/Feeder – A player who consistently dies in a match, often unintentionally helping the opposing team by giving them an advantage.
  • Farm – Repeatedly collecting resources, experience points, or in-game currency. Often refers to killing minions for gold and experience.
  • Flank – A tactic where players approach the enemy from an unexpected angle, typically from the sides or behind, to catch them off guard.

G

  • GG – Good Game; a polite expression used after a match, regardless of the result, to show respect to the other players.
  • Griefing – Intentionally sabotaging a teammate or the game environment for personal amusement or to frustrate others, often by trolling or intentionally throwing the game.
  • Glitch – A software bug or unintended behavior in the game that can give players an advantage or disrupt the normal gameplay.
  • Grind – The act of repetitively completing tasks in a game, often to gain experience, level up, or earn in-game currency.
  • Gank – An ambush by multiple players on a single player, typically in MOBA games, where one player is overwhelmed and killed.

H

  • HP – Hit Points; the health or life of a character. Once HP reaches zero, the character is usually eliminated or "killed."
  • Headshot – A critical shot that hits the enemy in the head, dealing significant or lethal damage. Common in shooter games.
  • Hacker – A player who uses third-party software or cheats to gain an unfair advantage in a game, often disrupting gameplay.
  • Hard Counter – A strategy or character that is specifically designed to defeat another, often negating its advantages.

I

  • IRL – In Real Life; used to distinguish between the virtual world of gaming and real-world events or activities.
  • Idle – A player who is not participating in a match or is AFK, often leading to penalties in some games.
  • Instakill – An attack or ability that kills an opponent instantly, typically without any chance to retaliate.
  • I-frame – Invincibility Frame; a brief period during which a character cannot be damaged, often after performing a specific action like dodging or using a special move.

J

  • Jungle – Refers to the area of a map in games like League of Legends where neutral monsters spawn. "Junglers" are players who focus on this area for farming and providing map control.
  • Juking – The act of rapidly changing direction or moving unpredictably to confuse enemies, often to avoid being hit or caught.
  • Join – Refers to entering a game or session, often as part of multiplayer matches. "Join a lobby" means to participate in an available match.

K

  • K/D – Kill/Death ratio; a stat that shows the number of kills a player has versus the number of times they’ve died. A higher ratio typically indicates better performance.
  • Kiting – A strategy in which a player attacks from a distance while continually moving to avoid being hit by the enemy.
  • Kingmaker – A term used to describe a player or action that indirectly influences the outcome of a match, typically by setting up other players for success.
  • Knockback – A mechanic that pushes a character away from the source of an attack, often used in combat games to disrupt positioning.
  • Karma – A term used in some games to describe a player’s behavior or actions, often rewarding or punishing good or bad actions. It’s also the name of an in-game system where players’ actions are rated.

L

  • Lag – A delay between a player’s input and the game’s response, often caused by network issues or server instability. Lag can significantly affect gameplay, especially in fast-paced games.
  • Looter – A player who focuses on gathering items, gear, and resources within the game, often at the expense of engaging in combat.
  • Leaver – A player who exits a game before it ends, often penalized in ranked games for abandoning their team.
  • Low-Key – Used to describe something subtle or understated, often used in casual chats to express a more relaxed, non-competitive approach.

M

  • Meta – Most Effective Tactics Available; the current dominant strategies or characters in a game, often shaped by balance changes and player trends.
  • MVP – Most Valuable Player; the player who contributes the most to the success of the team, often recognized for their exceptional performance.
  • Micro – Refers to the fine control and individual management of in-game units or characters, such as positioning or quick decision-making.
  • Macro – Refers to the broader strategy and game management, such as resource management or positioning across the map.
  • Meme – A joke, image, or video that becomes popular in gaming culture, often referring to funny or relatable moments. Memes can spread across social media and forums.

N

  • Nerf – A game update or patch that weakens or downgrades a character, ability, or weapon, usually to balance the game and reduce dominance.
  • Noob (or Newb) – A new or inexperienced player who lacks knowledge or skill in a particular game. It can be used as an insult or jokingly in some contexts.
  • Negging – A tactic where a player subtly undermines or manipulates another, often seen in online communities but not widely accepted in esports.

O

  • OP – Overpowered; used to describe a character, ability, or weapon that is stronger than intended, creating an imbalance in gameplay.
  • OOM – Out of Mana; used in games where a character uses a resource (like mana or energy) to perform abilities. It indicates that the player can no longer use their powerful abilities.
  • Overtime – A phase in games like Rocket League or Overwatch, where the match continues past the regular time if the score is tied.
  • Objective – A task or goal within a game, such as capturing a point or defeating a boss, that drives the overall progress of a match or level.
  • One-shot – A situation where a player or character is killed with a single attack or hit, often due to high damage output or a critical hit.

P

  • PvP – Player vs Player; a type of gameplay where players compete against each other rather than against the environment.
  • PvE – Player vs Environment; a mode where players face off against AI-controlled enemies, often focusing on narrative and cooperative play.
  • Pro – Short for Professional, referring to high-skill players or those who compete at an official, organized level.
  • Poggers – An expression used to show excitement or approval, often after an impressive play or moment in-game.
  • Pwned – A slang term derived from "owned," used to describe thoroughly defeating or humiliating an opponent.

Q

  • Queue – The system that matches players together for a game or lobby, often used in competitive matchmaking.
  • Quick Play – A casual game mode where players can quickly join a match without the pressure of ranked competition, often used for fun or practice.
  • QTE – Quick Time Event; an in-game event where players must perform specific actions within a short time window to progress, commonly in story-driven games.
  • Quitter – A player who leaves a game early, often before the match is completed, resulting in a disadvantage to their team.
  • Quadra Kill – A rare achievement where a player kills four enemies in quick succession in certain games, often rewarded with special accolades.

R

  • Respawn – The act of a player returning to the game after dying, often in a specific location or after a set time.
  • Ragequit – When a player leaves a game in frustration or anger, often without completing the match, leaving their team at a disadvantage.
  • Raid – A large, often coordinated group event in which players take on high-level challenges together, such as fighting a powerful boss or completing a dungeon.
  • Rushing – A strategy where players quickly move to achieve a specific goal, often by attacking or advancing aggressively to surprise the enemy.
  • Roll – A term used in some RPGs, where a player "rolls" a dice to determine an outcome, such as determining loot or combat success.

S

  • Smurfing – When a highly skilled player intentionally creates a new or lower-level account to play against less experienced players, giving them an unfair advantage.
  • Spawn – The location or process of appearing in the game, often used to describe where characters or enemies respawn after being defeated.
  • Squad – A group of players who team up to play together in multiplayer games. Teams of players, especially in battle royale games like Fortnite or PUBG, are often referred to as squads.
  • Sneak Peek – A preview or early look at upcoming game content, often released before an official update or expansion.
  • Stream Sniping – When a player watches a live stream of another player and uses that information to gain an unfair advantage, such as targeting the streamer’s location or actions.

T

  • Tilted – A term used when a player becomes frustrated or emotionally upset, often leading to poor gameplay. A "tilted" player may make irrational decisions or mistakes.
  • TP – Teleport; used to describe the action of a player moving instantly from one location to another, either by a character ability or a teleportation item.
  • Tryhard – A term used for players who put in maximum effort, sometimes excessively, in order to win, often at the expense of fun or casual play.
  • Troll – A player who intentionally tries to annoy, provoke, or sabotage others in the game, often through disruptive behavior or deliberately losing.
  • Tank – A character or player in an MMORPG or MOBA who is designed to take a lot of damage and protect their teammates, often by drawing enemy fire away from more vulnerable characters.

U

  • UBER – A term used to describe an extremely powerful or overpowered character, item, or ability, often used humorously or sarcastically in reference to something that’s too strong.
  • Ultimate – A special, often game-changing ability that a character can use in a match, typically after charging or cooldown. In Overwatch, for example, each hero has a unique Ultimate ability.
  • Uninstall – A phrase used humorously or sarcastically when a player is so frustrated with the game or another player’s performance that they suggest uninstalling the game entirely.
  • Underleveled – Refers to a character or player who has not reached an optimal level or strength compared to others, often leading to a disadvantage in the game.

V

  • VOD – Video on Demand; a recorded version of a live stream or game broadcast, often used for players to watch their own or others’ gameplay to improve skills.
  • Vanguard – A leading player or team member who is at the forefront of the action, often representing the offensive or tank role in team compositions.
  • Vent – Short for ventilation system, a term used in Among Us where players use vents to move around the map secretly, especially impostors who want to avoid being caught.
  • Victory Royale – A term used in Fortnite to signify winning a battle royale match, where only one player or squad remains standing at the end.

W

  • Wipe – A term used when a team is completely eliminated or defeated, often used in raid or boss fights where the entire group dies due to poor strategy or difficulty.
  • Win Streak – A consecutive series of victories, often used to describe a player or team that’s on a hot streak and winning several matches in a row.
  • Wallbang – The act of shooting or hitting an enemy through a wall or other environmental obstacle, commonly seen in tactical shooters.
  • Wombo Combo – A term used in fighting games, particularly in Super Smash Bros., where a combination of attacks is executed in a fast sequence, usually resulting in a devastating blow.

X

  • XP – Experience Points; a system used in many games to track a player’s progress, often used to level up characters or unlock new abilities or content.
  • X-factor – A player’s unique skill or ability that makes them stand out in a game, often referring to a player who can perform under pressure or in unexpected situations.

Y

  • Yoyoing – A term used to describe a player whose performance fluctuates wildly, often going from playing exceptionally well to performing poorly in rapid succession.
  • Yank – A slang term for removing or pulling out something in a game, such as “yanking” a character from a fight or pulling an ability too early.
  • Yasuo – A popular champion in League of Legends, often used to refer to players who are particularly good or bad with this character, due to his high skill ceiling and complex mechanics.

Z

  • Zero Ping – A network term that refers to a player having no delay between their actions and the server’s response, often ideal for competitive gameplay.
  • Zone – To control or dominate a specific area of the game map, often used in games like Battle Royale or MOBA titles.
  • Zap – The sound or action of a character using an electric attack, often used to describe abilities that deal electric or energy-based damage.
