Streaming & Content in Esports

General Information Popular Streamers Esports Content

Overview

From competitive esports stages to global streaming platforms, a new breed of digital icons has emerged—streamers who transcend gameplay to shape pop culture. These creators have built loyal followings through skill, personality, and a deep connection with their communities. Whether through high-stakes tournaments or candid livestreams, their influence extends beyond entertainment, redefining what it means to be a modern celebrity. Meet the pioneers who have become household names in gaming and streaming.

Tyson Ngo AKA TenZ

Tyson Ngo (born May 5, 2001), is a Canadian online streamer and former professional Valorant and Counter-Strike: Global Offensive player. He began his esports career in October 2019 with Cloud9 in CS:GO before transitioning to Valorant in April 2020, becoming the organization's first pro in the game. In January 2021, he stepped back from professional play to focus on streaming, but soon returned when he was loaned to Sentinels in April after a player suspension. He went on to help Sentinels win the VCT Stage Two Masters in Reykjavík and earned the Grand Finals MVP title.

On June 2, 2021, Sentinels bought out his Cloud9 contract for a reported $1.25 to $1.5 million. He remained with Sentinels until officially retiring from pro Valorant in September 2024. Ngo began content creation full-time in January 2020 and built a successful streaming career on Twitch. His quick rise, mechanical skill, and influence helped shape Valorant’s early competitive scene and streaming community.

Kyedae Alicia Shymko AKA Kyedae

Kyedae Alicia Shymko (born December 1, 2001) is a Canadian-Japanese Twitch streamer, content creator, and personality signed to 100 Thieves. She first gained visibility in late 2020 by appearing in TenZ’s streams and videos before growing her own audience through Valorant content. Kyedae stood out for her raw humor, relatability, and open journey in skill development, quickly becoming one of the most prominent female streamers in Valorant. She officially joined 100 Thieves in April 2021 as a content creator.

Originally a biology student at the University of British Columbia, she paused her studies to pursue content creation full-time. Her Twitch channel features a mix of high-energy gameplay and candid personal discussions about mental health and gaming culture. Kyedae also served as on-air talent at Valorant Champions Tour (VCT) events, earning acclaim for her charisma and insight. In 2024, she was nominated for Best Valorant Streamer and Most Inspiring Personality.

Imane Anys AKA Pokimane

Imane Anys (born May 14, 1996), known online as Pokimane, is a Moroccan-Canadian streamer who rose to fame for her engaging personality and League of Legends content. She created her Twitch account in 2013 after reaching Platinum rank in LoL and used a $250 PC to begin streaming. Her online name is a play on Pokémon and her real name, Imane. By 2017, she had 450,000 followers and was recognized as one of the top 100 most-followed Twitch channels.

In 2017, she won Best Twitch Streamer at the Shorty Awards and even appeared in a League of Legends trailer. Pokimane expanded her content with Fortnite, participating in the 2018 E3 Pro-Am event alongside NBA player Josh Hart. Despite her Fortnite success, she later scaled back her involvement, citing a desire to explore new content avenues. Her career has been marked by thoughtful evolution, transparency, and continued influence in the streaming industry.

Michael Grzesiek AKA Shroud

Michael Grzesiek (born June 2, 1994), better known as Shroud, is a Canadian streamer and former professional CS:GO and Valorant player. After retiring from CS:GO in April 2018, he became a full-time Twitch streamer and quickly climbed the platform’s ranks. In March 2019, he had over 100,000 subscribers—more than double the next top streamer. Later that year, he signed an exclusive contract with Mixer and was awarded Content Creator of the Year at The Game Awards 2019.

Following Mixer’s shutdown in 2020, Shroud declined a Facebook Gaming offer, returned to Twitch, and hit 516,000 peak viewers on his comeback stream. He won Gamer of the Year at the 2022 Streamer Awards and earned the Legacy Award in 2024. Shroud briefly returned to competition in 2022, joining Sentinels' Valorant team for the NA Last Chance Qualifier. His debut drew over 600,000 viewers, breaking records for a non-international Valorant match.

Richard Tyler Blevins AKA Ninja

Richard Tyler Blevins (born June 5, 1991), known online as Ninja, is an American streamer and professional gamer who began competing in Halo 3 in 2009. He played for top esports teams like Cloud9 and Luminosity Gaming before shifting to streaming full-time. His career exploded in 2017 when he began playing Fortnite, boosting his Twitch following from 500,000 to over 2 million in six months. By March 2018, he broke viewership records streaming with Drake and became the first to hit 3 million Twitch followers.

Ninja’s mainstream appeal led to brand deals, a Red Bull partnership, and a Fortnite skin. He left Twitch for Mixer in 2019 but returned after Mixer’s shutdown in 2020. In September 2022, he surprised fans with a break and returned simulcasting across Twitch, YouTube, TikTok, and other platforms. This move marked a shift toward cross-platform dominance and further solidified his legacy as one of the most influential streamers in gaming history.
