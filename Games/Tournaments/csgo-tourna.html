CS:GO Majors

CS:GO Majors: The Flagship Events of Counter-Strike Esports

The CS:GO Majors were inaugurated in 2013 with DreamHack Winter, organized in partnership with Valve, the developer of Counter-Strike: Global Offensive. The event featured a $250,000 prize pool, fully funded by Valve, which at the time was considered groundbreaking in the Counter-Strike scene. Since then, the Majors have grown in both scale and prestige, becoming the pinnacle of competitive CS:GO. Valve typically sponsors two Major tournaments per year (though in some years only one is held), each serving as a globally recognized proving ground for the best teams in the world. The prize pool has increased to $1,000,000 USD per Major, and the qualification structure has evolved into a rigorous multi-stage process involving open qualifiers, Regional Major Rankings (RMR), and a multi-tiered tournament format.

Organizer / Governing Body

Each Major is hosted by a third-party tournament organizer (e.g., ESL, PGL, BLAST) in partnership with Valve Corporation, which provides official sanctioning, funding, and oversight.

Games Featured in the Tournament

Only one game is featured: Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (and post-2023, Counter-Strike 2), a tactical first-person shooter renowned for its skill-based gameplay, deep economy mechanics, and iconic legacy in competitive gaming.

Tournament Format

The Major Championship in Counter-Strike features 24 teams who qualify through Regional Major Ranking (RMR) events across Europe, the Americas, and Asia-Pacific. Based on their RMR performance, teams are seeded into Legends, Challengers, or Contenders. The tournament opens with the Challengers Stage, where 16 teams compete in a Swiss format, with the top eight moving to the Legends Stage.

In the Legends Stage, those eight join the pre-seeded Legends, repeating the Swiss format to determine the final eight for the Champions Stage. The Champions Stage is a single-elimination playoff with all matches, including the Grand Final, played as best-of-three. Held entirely on LAN, the Major provides a low-latency, high-stakes environment that celebrates global talent, strategic variety, and a thrilling live experience for fans.

Prize Pool

Since 2016, the standard prize pool for each CS:GO Major has been $1,000,000 USD, funded entirely by Valve. In addition to the direct prize pool, teams also earn substantial revenue from in-game sticker sales, including team logos and player autographs, which can sometimes exceed their prize earnings. The prize distribution is typically structured as follows: first place receives approximately $500,000, second place earns around $150,000, while the third to eighth place teams receive between $35,000 and $70,000 depending on their final standings. Even teams eliminated in earlier stages are compensated, with payouts ranging from the ninth to the 24th position, ensuring a more sustainable competitive environment for all participating teams.

Live Streams and Coverage

CS:GO Majors provide extensive global broadcast coverage, allowing fans from every corner of the world to experience high-level competitive action. Official streams and community-driven broadcasts are available across multiple platforms, offering a variety of viewing options. With wide language support and expert-driven commentary, the Majors remain one of the most accessible and immersive events in esports.

Streaming Platforms

  • Twitch (official streams hosted by tournament organizers such as ESL, PGL, and BLAST)
  • YouTube Gaming (select events and archives)
  • Steam.TV (occasionally used by Valve for special broadcasts)

Languages and Regions

  • English
  • Russian
  • Portuguese
  • Mandarin
  • And more, tailored to various regional audiences

Venue and Production

The Major's production is world-class, featuring highly detailed stage designs, spectacular lighting setups, and impressive visual effects that amplify the intensity of each match. The broadcast’s immersive storytelling, alongside player highlights and team rivalries, engages both the hardcore fans and casual viewers, ensuring the Major remains a premier global esports event that attracts wide attention.

Global Stage

The Counter-Strike Major Championship is held in cities with strong esports cultures and advanced facilities, such as Katowice, Poland, and Stockholm, Sweden, with past editions also in locations like London, UK, and Cologne, Germany. Each venue is carefully selected to accommodate the large-scale production and passionate global audience. These cities contribute to the championship's reputation, drawing tens of thousands of spectators and offering an unmatched atmosphere for both players and fans.
