EVO Champion Series

Evolution Championship Series (EVO): The Premier Fighting Game Tournament

EVO began as a grassroots fighting game event called Battle by the Bay in 1996, held in California, USA. It rebranded to the Evolution Championship Series in 2002 and has since grown into the most prestigious and longest-running fighting game tournament in the world. Initially developed by the fighting game community (FGC) with a volunteer-driven ethos, EVO has maintained its identity as a celebration of player skill and competitive spirit. In 2021, Sony Interactive Entertainment and RTS (a subsidiary of Endeavor) acquired a joint stake in EVO, bringing more corporate structure while retaining the open-entry and community-led roots.

EVO is renowned for its inclusivity — allowing both amateurs and professionals to compete on the same stage — and for featuring multiple fighting game titles each year. It is known for iconic moments in esports history, such as “EVO Moment #37” involving Daigo Umehara and Justin Wong. It continues to serve as the definitive battleground for the global FGC.

Organizer/Governing Body

EVO is jointly operated by Sony Interactive Entertainment and RTS, with continued collaboration from tournament organizers and game publishers such as Capcom, Bandai Namco, Arc System Works, and SNK.

Games Featured in the Tournament

EVO is a multi-title event that features several of the most competitive and popular fighting games. While the game lineup can vary from year to year, recent editions of EVO have included titles such as Street Fighter VI (Capcom), Tekken 7 and Tekken 8 (Bandai Namco), Guilty Gear: Strive (Arc System Works), Mortal Kombat 1 (NetherRealm Studios), Dragon Ball FighterZ (Bandai Namco), King of Fighters XV (SNK), Melty Blood: Type Lumina (TYPE-MOON/French Bread), and Granblue Fantasy Versus: Rising (Cygames). This variety ensures that EVO remains a diverse and exciting tournament, bringing together the best players from multiple fighting game communities.

Tournament Format

EVO features an open-entry format, welcoming thousands of competitors from around the world across multiple fighting games. Unlike invitationals or league-based formats, EVO allows anyone to register, placing amateurs and pros in the same double-elimination bracket. Each tournament can host well over a thousand players, with early rounds organized into regional pools to reduce travel imbalance and improve competitive fairness.

Each pool operates on a double-elimination basis, with players needing two losses to be eliminated. Pool winners progress through bracket stages—typically Top 96 or Top 48, then Top 24, and finally Top 8, depending on the game’s turnout. Top 8 matches are played on the main stage in front of live audiences, complete with full-scale production, while match formats shift from best-of-3 in earlier rounds to best-of-5 in Top 8, demanding stamina and adaptability from the competitors.

Prize Pool

Prize pools at EVO are community-supported and publisher-boosted, with figures ranging from $10,000 to over $70,000 USD per title. Games like Street Fighter VI and Tekken 7/8 often exceed these figures due to sponsorships and developer bonuses. For example, Street Fighter VI in 2023 had a prize pool exceeding $70,000 USD, supported by Capcom and community crowd-funding via merchandise and DLC.

. Payouts are generally distributed among the Top 8 finalists, with 1st place receiving approximately 40%–50%, 2nd place around 20%–25%, and the remainder divided incrementally among lower placements. Some games may also award hardware or travel stipends to finalists from underrepresented regions.

Live Streams and Coverage

EVO streams primarily on Twitch, with additional coverage on YouTube Gaming and the official EVO website. Commentary is available in English, Japanese, Spanish, Portuguese, Korean, and other languages, with international restreams and community co-streaming enhancing global accessibility. The tournament features high-energy production, including LED displays, hype video intros, live audience reactions, and character-themed overlays.

EVO is hosted in cities with vibrant gaming cultures and world-class event venues, such as Las Vegas, USA, and Los Angeles, USA. These locations have been chosen for their accessibility and appeal to the global fighting game community, making each EVO tournament a significant event in the esports calendar. With a rich history and widespread fan base, these venues contribute to EVO’s reputation as the premier fighting game championship, attracting both players and spectators from around the world.
